# IT Support / Help Desk / Systems Administration

Part of domains/resume/industries/. See README.md in this folder for how this fits together. Load alongside domains/resume/patterns.md. New in July 2026, when the former combined tech.md was split; that file named this field in its title and covered none of it.

## The Canonical Tell

"Provided technical support to end users and resolved issues in a timely manner." It is the flagged bad example across 2026 help-desk resume guidance, and it is a definition rather than a claim. Every person who has held the title did that. *Timely* is the giveaway: it is a quantity word chosen specifically to avoid stating a quantity.

## This Field Has the Best Numbers in This Folder, and Resumes Omit Them

The unusual thing about IT support is that the work is already instrumented. Every ticket system records volume, resolution time, and SLA attainment automatically, so unlike administrative work (see administrative.md, where quantification is genuinely hard), the numbers exist and the candidate can look them up. A vague support resume is therefore a stronger tell than a vague resume in most other fields: the specifics were sitting in a dashboard and were not used.

The anchors that carry a bullet here:

- **Ticket volume**, per day or per week, and the queue it came from
- **First-call or first-contact resolution rate**, the field's most-read single number
- **SLA target and attainment**, stated as the target and the result, since attainment alone means nothing without the target
- **MTTR** in minutes or hours
- **Population supported**, users and endpoints, which sets the scale of everything else
- **CSAT**, where it was actually measured
- **Uptime**, for anything on the infrastructure side

One caution that applies harder here than elsewhere: these numbers are easy to inflate and are exactly the kind a rewrite supplies when none was given. See domains/resume/fabrication.md on the supplied number. A first-call resolution rate above roughly 90% is a real achievement in some environments and implausible in others, and the difference is the queue, not the candidate.

## Named Category Instead of Named System

The same principle as every other file in this folder. *Various ticketing systems*, *support tools*, *industry-standard platforms* match nothing. ServiceNow, Jira Service Management, Zendesk, Freshservice; Active Directory, Entra ID, Intune, Jamf, SCCM; Microsoft 365 admin, Okta, VPN and MDM by name.

Name the environment as well as the tool. Windows 11, macOS fleet, hybrid AD, and which of those the candidate actually administered rather than only used, since that distinction is the difference between two different jobs with the same title.

## Tier Is Not a Synonym for Seniority

A field-specific version of scope inflation, and one a rewrite introduces easily. Tier 1, 2, and 3 describe escalation depth, and organizations define them differently. A resume that says "Tier 3 support" without saying what escalated to it is claiming a level rather than describing work. The checkable version names what the candidate was the last stop for.

Related: *supported*, *administered*, *managed*, and *owned* are four different relationships to a system, and only the candidate knows which applies. This is the widened verb from fabrication.md, and this field's version of it is unusually easy to introduce because the words are near-synonyms in ordinary speech and are not near-synonyms to a hiring manager.

## This Field's "Best Practices"

**"Excellent customer service skills"** and **"strong troubleshooting ability."** Both are the field's version of the empty authoritative phrase, and both are unfalsifiable claims about a person rather than statements about work. They are also the two phrases most likely to appear in the summary line of an AI-written support resume, because they are what the job description asked for, restated.

The replacement is a behavior with a number attached: a satisfaction score, a repeat-contact rate, or a specific hard conversation that ended somewhere. *Troubleshooting* becomes the actual diagnostic path on the actual recurring problem.

## Documentation and Automation Are the Advancement Signal

The bullet that separates a support resume from a systems resume, and the one most often left off entirely. Knowledge-base articles written, runbooks produced, a recurring ticket class eliminated at the root, a manual process scripted. Ticket-closing volume shows capacity; removing a ticket class shows judgment, and hiring for anything above the front line reads for the second.

Concretely: "wrote the onboarding checklist the team still uses" and "scripted the account-provisioning step that was eating twenty minutes per new hire" both describe work that outlasted the shift, which is what the reader is looking for and what a generic support resume never contains.

## Fabrication Risk

Extends domains/resume/fabrication.md into this field's easy overstatements:

- **Metrics no one measured.** Many small environments do not track FCR or CSAT at all. A resume from such a place carrying precise figures for both is describing a dashboard that did not exist.
- **Tier level**, as above.
- **Administered versus used.** Having an admin console open is not administering the system.
- **Certification status**, where CompTIA A+, Network+, and vendor certifications expire or sit in progress, and the acronym does not change when they lapse.

## Worked Example

**Before:**
"Provided technical support to end users and resolved issues in a timely manner. Utilized various ticketing systems to track and document incidents. Demonstrated excellent customer service skills and strong troubleshooting ability in a fast-paced environment."

**After:**
"Tier 1 and 2 for ~1,400 users across three sites, 60 to 80 ServiceNow tickets a week, 91% first-contact resolution against a 15-minute response SLA. Mostly M365 provisioning, VPN, and Windows 11 login failures. Wrote the runbook for the profile-corruption issue that had been bouncing to Tier 3 twice a month; it stopped escalating."

Every anchor is present and none is decorative: population, volume, the tool by name, FCR against a stated target, the actual ticket mix, and one thing that outlasted the shift. The last sentence is the strongest line in the bullet, because it is the only part describing judgment rather than capacity, and it is the part a generic rewrite would never invent.
