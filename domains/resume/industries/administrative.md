# Administrative / Office Support

Part of domains/resume/industries/. See README.md in this folder for how this fits together. Load alongside domains/resume/patterns.md.

## Why This Field Is Harder to Write Specifically For

"Administrative assistant" covers wildly different actual jobs, a legal admin using case-management software and deposition terminology has almost nothing in common day-to-day with an executive assistant managing a CEO's calendar or a small-office generalist doing a bit of everything. The shared title suggests more overlap than exists. That gap makes generic phrasing both easier to fall into (there's no obvious tech stack or framework to name instead) and easier to catch (a resume that never mentions which kind of administrative work it actually was is doing the vague-category trick in its purest form).

## Named Software Instead of "Proficient in Technology"

The direct parallel to cybersecurity.md's named-tool principle. "Proficient in various office technologies" matches nothing an ATS or a reviewer is searching for. Excel, Outlook, Salesforce, QuickBooks, SharePoint: naming the actual tool is the entire fix, and it costs nothing to do.

## The Soft-Skill Buzzword Density Problem

This field sees the heaviest concentration of domains/resume/patterns.md's generic Tier 1 list (detail-oriented, team player, organized, hardworking) of any industry covered so far, largely because the work itself is harder to quantify by nature than sales or engineering. That makes the fix more important here, not less: "organized" becomes "maintained a filing and scheduling system for 40+ active client files with zero missed deadlines over two years," a specific claim built from the same unglamorous work, not a different job.

## The Cross-Document Consistency Check

Worth a specific mention here because it comes up often in this field: a resume that reads competently generic, then a cover letter or interview that reveals a completely different voice, is its own tell independent of anything on the page. Same principle as domains/resume/patterns.md's Cover Letters section, restated because reviewers in administrative hiring specifically flag it as a common giveaway.

## Worked Example

**Before:**
"Detail-oriented administrative professional and team player with excellent communication skills. Proficient in various office technologies and dedicated to supporting organizational efficiency. Managed calendars and coordinated meetings for senior leadership."

**After:**
"Supported three partners at a 12-attorney immigration firm. Ran the filing calendar for roughly 40 active cases, tracking USCIS receipt dates and response deadlines in Clio; no missed deadline in two years. Rebuilt the intake packet in plain language after clients kept returning the old one incomplete, which cut follow-up calls to about a third of what they were."

This is the hardest field to write specifically for, so note what carried the weight: not a metric bolted onto a generic claim, but naming which kind of administrative work it actually was. Immigration filing deadlines are a different job from executive calendar management, and the "before" version conceals which one this candidate has done. "Detail-oriented" is not replaced by a synonym; it is replaced by two years of deadlines without a miss, which is the evidence the adjective was gesturing at.
