# Presentations and Slide Decks

Part of domains/. Different from domains/fiction.md in one important way: this domain mostly doesn't need new machinery. It's the same patterns already in this skill, showing up one level up, at the slide instead of the sentence or paragraph. Load alongside core/structural-patterns.md and domains/design.md; this file is the slide-specific instantiation of both, not a replacement for either.

---

## The Stakes

A 2025 Stanford HCI study found viewers could identify an AI-generated presentation within the first three slides 71% of the time, usually without being able to say exactly why. That "usually without knowing why" detail matters: the tell is rarely one bad slide, it's the cumulative pattern across several.

## This Is a Hybrid Medium

A deck is text and image doing the same job side by side, and AI slop shows up on both sides independently. Financial projections and data claims often arrive as chart images, not text, worth remembering since a text-only read of a deck can miss exactly the slide most likely to be padded (see Investor and Pitch Decks below).

## Cross-Medium Confirmations, Not New Discoveries

The single most-cited AI tell in current deck-critique writing is this: every bullet the same length, three to four bullets per slide, each one starting with a verb. That's Rule of Three and this skill's bullet-opener patterns, just one level up from where they usually get caught. A few more slide-level instances of patterns already in this skill:

- **Identical layout, slide after slide, just the text swapped.** This is domains/design.md's Section-Layout-Repetition Rule, verbatim, just at deck scale instead of page scale.
- **The same narrow icon set and stock-photo style across completely unrelated decks.** domains/design.md's hand-rolled-icon and generic-imagery notes, same mechanism.
- **Headings and content with nothing specific to the actual company or team.** Untailored Is the Real Tell from domains/resume/patterns.md, restated for slides: swap the logo and nothing else would need to change.

None of this needed new philosophy to explain. It needed slide-specific examples, which is most of what this file actually adds.

## Investor and Pitch Decks

The one area with genuinely new, sharp, current material, not just an old pattern in new clothes.

**"Proprietary technology" or "proprietary AI"** as a moat claim is this domain's version of the security section's "improved security posture": a phrase any competitor could paste into their own deck unchanged. The fix names the actual mechanism, a data flywheel, a specific workflow integration, a distribution advantage, not the category of having one.

**Top-down market sizing is now itself the tell.** A TAM/SAM/SOM slide built from a big Gartner number pulled off the internet is recognized as generic in 2026, precisely because it's the easiest slide for a model to generate without any real information about the business. The credible version builds bottom-up: this many realistic customers, at this price, times this realistic share, arrived at from the company's own numbers, not a market report.

**"Why now" needs a named, specific catalyst, not a mood.** Pre-2022 versions of this slide gestured at macro trends. The credible version names the one technical, regulatory, or market shift that makes this specific company fundable this quarter and wouldn't have made it fundable two years ago.

**The company-name test, independently confirmed.** A deck that "sounds correct but interchangeable," where swapping the company's name wouldn't require changing much else, is the exact same untailored-content tell already established for resumes and LinkedIn profiles, just showing up in a fundraising context now. Same test works: read the problem slide, the differentiation slide, and the go-to-market slide, and ask whether they could describe a different company with a find-and-replace.

Practical calibration: even practitioners who use AI to draft pitch decks report expecting to rewrite 30 to 50 percent of the first pass before it's specific enough to send. Treat that as the realistic floor, not a worst case.

## Speaker Notes and Narration

Mostly the same specificity principle again, applied to what gets said out loud rather than what's on the slide: generic prompts produce generic narration, and the fix current tools themselves recommend, replace generic examples with specifics only the actual presenter would know, is Specificity Erasure's fix restated for a spoken context. Not sharp enough or different enough a mechanism to warrant its own pattern category here.

## Workflow Principle

Same finding as domains/fiction.md and the LinkedIn section of domains/resume/patterns.md, confirmed again in a third medium: an outline reviewed and corrected before slides get generated catches problems far more cheaply than editing twenty finished slides after the fact. Fix the structure while it's still an outline, not after.
