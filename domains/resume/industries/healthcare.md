# Healthcare — Nursing / Allied Health / Clinical

Part of domains/resume/industries/. See README.md in this folder for how this fits together. Load alongside domains/resume/patterns.md.

## The Canonical Tell

"Provided compassionate, patient-centered care." It appears as the flagged bad example across independent 2026 nursing-resume guides, and it fails twice over. Compassionate is unfalsifiable, and providing patient care is the definition of the job rather than a claim about this candidate. It describes every nurse who has ever held the license, which is what makes it worth nothing to the person reading it.

The specific version carries acuity, ratio, and modality: "Managed 2:1 assignments in a cardiac ICU on mechanical ventilation, IABP, and CRRT" tells a clinical hiring manager the scope of practice in about five seconds, and none of it is guessable from the generic version.

## Named Category Instead of Named System

The same mechanism as cybersecurity.md's named-tool principle, with a field-specific vocabulary. "Experienced with electronic health records" matches nothing; Epic, Cerner, Meditech, and Pyxis do. The category words to watch here are *healthcare systems*, *clinical technologies*, *various units*, and *diverse patient populations*, each standing in for a proper noun that exists and could have been named.

Unit and population are part of the same test. "ICU experience" is a category; medical, surgical, cardiac, neuro, and pediatric ICUs are different jobs, and a resume that never says which is doing the vague-category trick in a field where the distinction is the whole qualification.

## This Field's "Best Practices"

**"Evidence-based practice."** Structurally identical to the problem cybersecurity.md flags with *best practices*: reached for to sound rigorous while naming no evidence and no practice. It becomes a real claim when it points at a specific protocol, a bundle, a measured change, or the guideline actually followed. Until then it is a phrase every clinician could write about every shift they have ever worked.

*Patient-centered*, *holistic*, and *interdisciplinary* behave the same way in this field and deserve the same treatment.

## Numeric Camouflage

The field's characteristic version attaches a real number to an unnamed instrument. "Improved patient satisfaction scores by 15%" does not say which instrument, over what period, against what baseline, or across how many beds. Satisfaction is measured by named surveys with named domains, so the specific claim is available and the vague one is a choice. Same question as everywhere else in this skill: what standard, whose framework, which system, measured how.

## Fabrication Risk: Licensure Is Legally Checkable

A direct and sharper extension of patterns.md's Fabrication Risk, and the reason this file exists as much as the vocabulary does. Clinical credentials are verified against state boards and primary sources as a condition of employment, so an overstatement here is not a resume problem that gets discovered in an interview. It is a licensure matter with regulatory consequences, and in most jurisdictions misrepresenting a credential is actionable independent of whether anyone was hired.

The specific things a model will smooth over when asked to strengthen a clinical resume, none of which it was told mattered:

- A license listed without state, or without noting whether it is a compact multistate license
- A certification listed without an expiration date when it has one (BLS, ACLS, PALS all expire)
- A specialty certification stated as held when it is in progress or lapsed (CCRN, CEN, OCN)
- Scope of practice widened by a verb: *performed* where the candidate assisted, *managed* where the candidate monitored

Flag every one of these and ask. Do not resolve them silently, and do not let a rewrite pass introduce them.

## Worked Example

**Before:**
"Provided compassionate, patient-centered care to diverse patient populations. Utilized electronic health records to document patient information. Collaborated with interdisciplinary teams to improve patient outcomes and satisfaction scores."

**After:**
"Charge nurse on a 24-bed cardiac ICU, 2:1 and 1:1 assignments, ventilated and post-CABG patients. Documented in Epic; served on the unit's Pyxis discrepancy review after a recurring night-shift count issue. Ran the handoff-script pilot that cut missed telemetry alarms from 9 a week to 2 over four months."

Every clinical detail in the "after" version answers a question the "before" version raised and dropped: which unit, what acuity, which system, which team, what actually changed. The alarm figure is small and unrounded, which is what a real measured result usually looks like. Note also what the rewrite did not do: it did not convert "collaborated with teams" into a leadership claim, because nothing in the source said the candidate led anything. See domains/resume/fabrication.md on the widened verb.
