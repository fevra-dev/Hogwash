# Phrases — Banned Patterns by Category

---

## ELIMINATE ON SIGHT — Tier 1

These are unambiguous AI signals in virtually all contexts.

### Throat-Clearing Openers
Delete. Start with the content.

- "In today's [fast-paced / rapidly evolving / competitive / digital] world..."
- "In the age of [AI / digital transformation / big data]..."
- "It's worth noting that..." / "It's important to note..."
- "It goes without saying..." *(also ban mid-sentence)*
- "Needless to say..."
- "Let's dive in." / "Let's explore." / "Let's get started."
- "Here's what you need to know."
- "Without further ado..."
- "At the end of the day..."
- "At its core..."
- "In today's competitive landscape..."
- "In this article, we will..." / "In this post, I'll cover..."
- "When it comes to [X]..." → start with X
- "In the realm of [X]..." → in X
- "Ever-evolving landscape" / "ever-changing world"
- "Here's the thing:" → delete, just say the thing
- "Let me be clear:" → delete, just be clear
- "Let that sink in." → delete
- "The uncomfortable truth is..." → state the truth
- Sentences starting with "So," or "Look," as openers to add gravitas

### Sycophantic Openers
Delete. Respond directly.

"Great question!" · "That's a fascinating question." · "Absolutely!" · "Certainly!" · "Of course!" · "Sure!" · "You're absolutely right!" · "You raise an excellent point." · "I'm glad you asked."

### Chatbot Artifacts
Delete entirely. These are leaked conversation-mode phrasing.

- "I hope this helps!"
- "Let me know if you have any questions."
- "Let me know if you need any modifications." *(specific leaked ChatGPT tail, highly diagnostic)*
- "Feel free to ask if you need clarification."
- "I'd be happy to help with that."
- "As an AI language model..." / "As an AI, I..."
- "I hope this email finds you well."
- "Is there anything else I can help you with today?"
- "Thank you for contacting us."

### Emphasis Crutches
AI uses these for false drama. Delete and state the substance directly.

- "That's it. That's the [noun]."
- "And that's okay."
- "The best part?"
- "And here's the kicker"
- "Enter: [thing]"
- "Ready to level up?"
- "Not because X. Because Y." *(staccato variant)*
- "The truth is..." / "The reality is..." / "Make no mistake..."
- "Here's the thing:" / "The fact of the matter is..."
- "What's clear is..." / "What this means is..."

### Knowledge Disclaimers
AI hedge phrases used when it doesn't know something. Delete or replace with honest uncertainty or a real source.

- "as of [date]" *(when hedging rather than citing a real source with genuine time-sensitivity)*
- "based on available information"
- "while specific details are limited"
- "it appears to suggest"
- "tends to" *(as a hedge, not a genuine statistical claim)*
- "may potentially" / "could possibly" / "might perhaps"
- "it could be argued that"
- "some might say"

### The Golden Sentence
If a sentence sounds designed to be screenshot and shared, a quotable aphorism, rewrite it. These are performative depth, not actual insight.
- "Innovation is not a destination; it is a journey."
- "The future belongs to those who build it."
- "Great software is never finished, only shipped."
- Pattern: any sentence that reads like a conference slide pull-quote.

## STRUCTURAL FORMULAS — Rewrite, don't just delete

These require understanding the pattern and replacing with specific content.

### Challenges-Future Prospects Boilerplate
Pattern: positive framing → "Despite this, [entity] faces challenges" (never named specifically) → vague optimism → speculative "Future Prospects."

Before: "Despite its industrial prosperity, Korattur faces challenges typical of urban areas, including traffic congestion. Despite these challenges, Korattur continues to thrive."
After: "Traffic congestion increased after 2015 when three new IT parks opened. The municipal corporation began a stormwater drainage project in 2022 to address recurring floods."

Fix: Name specific challenges. Name specific responses. Cut the optimism framing.

### Significance Inflation
Before: "The Statistical Institute of Catalonia was officially established in 1989, marking a pivotal moment in the evolution of regional statistics in Spain."
After: "The Statistical Institute of Catalonia was established in 1989 to collect and publish regional statistics independently from Spain's national statistics office."

Fix: The inflation, "marking a pivotal moment," adds nothing. Delete and replace with what the event actually meant.

### Vague Aims
- "aims to [verb]" → state what the thing does
- "is designed to [verb]" → just say what it does
- "seeks to [verb]" → does
- "works to [verb]" → does
- "maintains an active social media presence" → state actual reach or cut
- "expressed excitement" / "expressed enthusiasm" → name what they actually said
- "known for its [adjective]" → name a specific thing or cut
- "it is advisable to" → say who should do what
- "Further research is needed." → name what specific research would determine what specific outcome, or cut

### Vague Attribution
Name the source and year, or cut the claim entirely.

"Experts believe..." · "Studies show..." · "Research suggests..." · "Many people think..." · "It is widely known that..." · "Industry observers note..." · "objective studies aimed..." *(GPTZero 269x frequency multiplier in academic register)* · "according to some"

### Generic Conclusions
Replace with specific plans, facts, or positions. Or stop.

"The future looks bright." · "Only time will tell." · "Exciting times lie ahead." · "The possibilities are endless." · "This is just the beginning." · "We're only scratching the surface."

## HEDGING — Pick a position

### Hedging Seesaw
- "Not just X, but Y" → state Y directly
- "This isn't about X — it's about Y" → state what it is about
- "It's not X, it's Y" → state Y *(exception: acceptable when 2–3 sentences of expansion separate the negative and positive: the context-dependent exception. The tell is density, not absolute count)*
- "Not only X, but also Y" → state what matters
- "On one hand... on the other hand..." *(when no genuine tension exists)*
- "There are pros and cons" *(without stating them)*
- "And honestly?" *(simulated intimacy: appears at statistical rates, not genuine)*

### Dramatic Fragmentation
AI overuses one-sentence paragraphs for false drama. Reserve for genuine impact.

- One-sentence paragraphs at the end of every section: if this is a pattern, break it
- "X changed everything." → name what changed and how
- Short hook questions in isolation before answering them
- Staccato strings: "We built it. It worked. Everything shifted." *(fine once; a tell as a recurring rhythm)*

## BANNED SENTENCE OPENERS

These appear at statistically anomalous rates at sentence/paragraph starts. Restructure. Do not just move them mid-sentence.

`Certainly,` · `Moreover,` · `Furthermore,` · `Additionally,` · `Nevertheless,` · `Nonetheless,` · `In conclusion,` · `To summarize,` · `In summary,` · `Ultimately,` · `Notably,` *(all positions)* · `Importantly,` · `Interestingly,` · `Significantly,` · `However,` *(one per section maximum — more than one per 500 words is a tell)*

Better approaches:
- Often no transition is needed. Start the next thought
- Use the actual logical connection: "because," "so," "but," "and"
- Reference the previous idea directly instead of using a generic connector
- Let paragraph breaks do the transitional work

## BUSINESS JARGON

`circle back` → follow up · `take it offline` → discuss separately · `move the needle` → change the result · `low-hanging fruit` → easy wins · `deep dive` → look closely at · `30,000-foot view` → overview · `value proposition` → the offer · `rockstar` *(team descriptor)* · `synergize` · `bandwidth` *(meaning human capacity)* · `boil the ocean` → attempt too much

- "Thrilled to share..." / "Excited to announce..." / "Excited to share..."

## FILLER PHRASES — Quick Fixes

| Inflated | Direct |
|---|---|
| "In order to achieve this" | "To achieve this" |
| "Due to the fact that" | "Because" |
| "At this point in time" | "Now" |
| "In the event that" | "If" |
| "The system has the ability to" | "The system can" |
| "It is worth noting that the data shows" | "The data shows" |
| "For the purpose of" | "For" or "to" |
| "In the case of" | "For" |
| "Despite the fact that" | "Although" |
| "On a regular basis" | "Regularly" |
